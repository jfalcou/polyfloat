Precision

Representation

A floating-point expansion of dimension \(N\) represents a real number as the unevaluated sum \(x_0 + x_1 + \cdots + x_{N-1}\) of floating-point numbers of one type T, of decreasing magnitude, which do not overlap: two successive parts satisfy \(x_k + x_{k+1} = x_k\) in T, rounded to nearest, which implies \(|x_{k+1}| \le \mathrm{ulp}(x_k)/2\). plf::is_normalized tests this condition, which is stricter by a factor of 2 than the ulp-nonoverlapping expansions of Joldes, Marty, Muller, and Popescu, \(|x_{k+1}| \le \mathrm{ulp}(x_k)\).

The expansions of the literature have two, three, four, or more parts, and POLYFLOAT implements those of two and three parts, plf::double_real_t and plf::triple_real_t, over float and double. The arithmetic on the parts uses the hardware floating-point operations and the error-free transformations of Joldes, Marty, Muller, and Popescu.

Precision

An expansion of dimension \(N\) over T has \(N\) times the mantissa bits of T, which plf::effective_mantissa_bits returns. The exponent range is the one of T. These figures describe the representation, and do not bound the error of an operation.

Base type Dimension Mantissa bits \(2^{-\text{bits}}\) plf::eps
float 2 48 \(3.5527 \cdot 10^{-15}\) \(2^{-48}\)
float 3 72 \(2.1176 \cdot 10^{-22}\) \(2^{-72}\)
double 2 106 \(1.2326 \cdot 10^{-32}\) \(2^{-104}\)
double 3 159 \(1.3685 \cdot 10^{-48}\) \(2^{-156}\)

Over float, an expansion of dimension 3 exceeds the 53 bits of double, while one of dimension 2 falls short of them, and both keep the exponent range of float. They extend the precision on a target without double, or estimate the rounding error of a computation in float. Over double, both dimensions exceed the 64 bits of the x87 extended format, while the 113 bits of IEEE binary128, the long double of some targets, exceed the 106 bits of dimension 2. An expansion also applies to SIMD registers, which long double does not.

Limits

  • Overflow and underflow occur where they occur in T.
  • The low part becomes denormal when the high part falls below about \(2^{-969}\) for a plf::double_real_t<double>, and \(2^{-916}\) for a plf::triple_real_t<double>, and the precision decreases from there. Below the smallest positive normal value of T, the high part is itself denormal and has fewer bits than T.
  • The infinities and the quiet NaN are represented in the high part, the other parts being zero.
  • The distance from a finite value to the next representable one can be as small as the smallest denormal of T, so the representable values are not evenly spaced as in T.

Testing

A function is tested against its implementation in MPFR, when one exists. MPFR evaluates it with ten times the mantissa bits of the tested type, 1060 bits for plf::double_real_t<double>. Its result is converted to that type part by part: the high part is the result rounded to T, and each following part the remainder rounded to T. The reference carries a rounding error of its own, of the order of \(2^{-\text{bits}}\). The test passes when the distance between the two results is under a multiple of the \(2^{-\text{bits}}\) of the type, set by each test. The distance between \(a\) and \(b\), which plf::reldist computes, is

\[ \frac{|a - b|}{\max(|a|, |b|, 1)}, \]

the relative error when \(\max(|a|, |b|)\) exceeds 1, and the absolute error below. A result of magnitude below 1 is therefore tested against an absolute bound, and a result below that bound passes whatever its value.
